## Purpose
This procedure establishes standardized methods for collecting, documenting, and managing evidence during compliance audits. It ensures evidence is collected ethically, legally, and in accordance with professional auditing standards to support audit conclusions and regulatory compliance.

## Scope
This procedure applies to all compliance audits conducted within the organization, including internal audits, external audits, and regulatory examinations. It covers evidence collection for information security, data protection, financial compliance, and operational controls.

## Definitions

### Evidence Types
- **Testimonial Evidence**: Information obtained through interviews, surveys, or discussions
- **Physical Evidence**: Tangible items such as documents, logs, or system outputs
- **Documentary Evidence**: Policies, procedures, records, and other written materials
- **Analytical Evidence**: Results of analyses, calculations, or data evaluations
- **Observational Evidence**: Direct observations of processes, controls, or activities

### Evidence Quality Criteria
- **Sufficiency**: Adequate quantity to support audit conclusions
- **Appropriateness**: Relevant to the audit objective and scope
- **Reliability**: Obtained from trustworthy sources using proper methods
- **Validity**: Accurately represents the facts or conditions being audited

## Evidence Collection Planning

### Pre-Audit Planning
1. **Evidence Requirements Identification**
   - Review audit objectives and scope
   - Identify required evidence types for each control
   - Determine sampling methodologies
   - Plan evidence collection timing and sequence

2. **Resource Planning**
   - Identify required personnel and expertise
   - Arrange access to systems, facilities, and personnel
   - Prepare evidence collection tools and templates
   - Schedule interviews and observations

3. **Legal and Ethical Considerations**
   - Review data protection and privacy requirements
   - Obtain necessary permissions and authorizations
   - Plan for handling sensitive or confidential information
   - Ensure compliance with legal hold requirements

### Evidence Collection Strategy
- **Risk-Based Approach**: Focus on high-risk areas first
- **Sampling Strategy**: Define statistical or judgmental sampling methods
- **Documentation Strategy**: Establish evidence indexing and cross-referencing
- **Chain of Custody**: Plan for maintaining evidence integrity

## Evidence Collection Methods

### 1. Documentary Evidence Collection

#### Policy and Procedure Documents
**Collection Process:**
1. Request documents from process owners
2. Review document version control and approval dates
3. Assess document completeness and accuracy
4. Cross-reference with actual practices

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Document title, version, and approval date
- Source and custodian information
- Date and method of collection
- Audit team member who collected the evidence

#### System Logs and Records
**Collection Process:**
1. Identify relevant log sources and time periods
2. Request log exports from system administrators
3. Validate log integrity and completeness
4. Analyze log data for relevant patterns

**Technical Considerations:**
- Use secure transfer methods for log files
- Maintain original file hashes for integrity verification
- Document extraction parameters and filters used
- Ensure proper handling of sensitive log data

### 2. Testimonial Evidence Collection

#### Interview Procedures
**Planning Phase:**
- Identify key personnel and subject matter experts
- Prepare interview questions in advance
- Schedule interviews to minimize disruption
- Arrange appropriate meeting facilities

**Conducting Interviews:**
1. **Opening**: Explain audit purpose, confidentiality, and ground rules
2. **Questioning**: Use open-ended questions followed by specific probes
3. **Documentation**: Record responses in real-time with interviewee approval
4. **Closing**: Summarize key points and allow for clarifications

**Interview Documentation:**
- Interviewee name, title, and department
- Date, time, and location of interview
- List of attendees (audit team and interviewee)
- Signed interviewee acknowledgment of accuracy

#### Survey and Questionnaire Methods
**Design Phase:**
- Define survey objectives and target population
- Develop clear, unbiased questions
- Test survey for clarity and completeness
- Determine distribution and collection methods

**Administration:**
1. Distribute surveys with clear instructions
2. Track response rates and follow up as needed
3. Ensure anonymity if requested
4. Analyze responses for patterns and themes

### 3. Physical and Observational Evidence

#### Process Observation
**Planning:**
- Identify processes to be observed
- Coordinate with process owners for appropriate timing
- Prepare observation checklists and forms
- Arrange for unobtrusive observation methods

**Observation Process:**
1. **Pre-Observation**: Brief process owner on observation purpose
2. **During Observation**: Document actual vs. documented procedures
3. **Post-Observation**: Discuss findings with process owner
4. **Documentation**: Record observations with timestamps and context

**Observation Documentation:**
- Process or activity observed
- Date, time, and duration of observation
- Observer name and qualifications
- Detailed notes on what was observed
- Any deviations from documented procedures

#### Physical Evidence Collection
**Handling Procedures:**
1. Photograph or scan physical documents
2. Maintain chain of custody for physical items
3. Document condition and location of evidence
4. Secure evidence in appropriate storage

**Special Considerations:**
- Handle sensitive documents according to classification policies
- Obtain permission before photographing sensitive areas
- Document any alterations or movements of physical evidence

### 4. Analytical Evidence Collection

#### Data Analysis Procedures
**Data Collection:**
1. Identify data sources and required datasets
2. Request data extracts from system owners
3. Validate data completeness and accuracy
4. Perform initial data quality assessments

**Analysis Methods:**
- Statistical analysis for trends and anomalies
- Comparative analysis against benchmarks
- Root cause analysis for identified issues
- Correlation analysis for related controls

#### Automated Evidence Collection
**Tool Configuration:**
- Configure audit tools for appropriate data collection
- Set parameters for automated testing and monitoring
- Validate tool accuracy and reliability
- Document tool settings and configurations

**Automated Collection Types:**
- Log analysis and pattern detection
- Configuration compliance scanning
- Access control testing
- Vulnerability assessment results

## Evidence Documentation Standards

### Evidence Indexing System
- **Unique Identifier**: Assign sequential evidence numbers (EVD-001, EVD-002, etc.)
- **Cross-Reference**: Link evidence to specific audit procedures and findings
- **Metadata**: Include collection date, source, collector, and validation status
- **Version Control**: Track evidence updates and revisions

### Work Paper Standards
**Required Elements:**
- **Header Information**: Evidence ID, date collected, collector name
- **Purpose Statement**: Why this evidence was collected
- **Source Information**: Original location and custodian
- **Collection Method**: How evidence was obtained
- **Sufficiency Assessment**: Evaluation of evidence adequacy

**Documentation Formats:**
- **Narrative Descriptions**: Written explanations of findings
- **Screenshots and Photographs**: Visual documentation with annotations
- **Data Extracts**: Spreadsheet exports with filtering criteria
- **Checklists and Forms**: Standardized documentation templates

### Evidence Validation
**Validation Methods:**
- **Source Verification**: Confirm evidence comes from authoritative sources
- **Cross-Referencing**: Compare multiple evidence sources for consistency
- **Reperformance**: Independently verify calculations or processes
- **Expert Review**: Consult subject matter experts for technical validation

**Validation Documentation:**
- Validation method used
- Validator name and qualifications
- Date and results of validation
- Any discrepancies identified and resolved

## Evidence Management and Storage

### Storage Requirements
- **Secure Storage**: Use encrypted, access-controlled storage systems
- **Backup Procedures**: Regular backups with integrity verification
- **Retention Periods**: Maintain evidence for regulatory-required periods
- **Disposal Procedures**: Secure deletion when retention periods expire

### Access Controls
- **Need-to-Know Basis**: Limit access to authorized audit team members
- **Audit Trails**: Log all access to evidence files
- **Change Tracking**: Document any modifications to evidence
- **Chain of Custody**: Maintain custody records for physical evidence

### Evidence Integrity
**Integrity Controls:**
- **Hash Values**: Calculate and store cryptographic hashes
- **Digital Signatures**: Use digital signatures for critical evidence
- **Version Control**: Track all changes to evidence files
- **Backup Verification**: Regularly verify backup integrity

## Legal and Ethical Considerations

### Data Protection Compliance
- **Privacy Laws**: Ensure compliance with GDPR, CCPA, and other privacy regulations
- **Data Minimization**: Collect only necessary evidence
- **Purpose Limitation**: Use evidence only for audit purposes
- **Retention Limits**: Delete evidence when no longer needed

### Confidentiality Requirements
- **Sensitive Information**: Handle confidential data according to classification policies
- **Attorney-Client Privilege**: Respect legal privileges and work product
- **Non-Disclosure Agreements**: Honor existing confidentiality agreements
- **Information Barriers**: Maintain separation between conflicting audit areas

### Ethical Standards
- **Professional Conduct**: Adhere to IIA Code of Ethics and professional standards
- **Objectivity**: Maintain impartiality in evidence collection and evaluation
- **Competence**: Ensure auditors have appropriate skills and knowledge
- **Due Care**: Exercise professional judgment and care in all activities

- Identification of additional evidence needs

**Quality Control Checks:**
- Completeness: All required evidence types collected
- Accuracy: Evidence accurately represents facts
- Relevance: Evidence supports audit objectives
- Reliability: Evidence from trustworthy sources

### Supervisor Review
- **Technical Review**: Subject matter expert validation
- **Compliance Review**: Legal and regulatory compliance check
- **Methodology Review**: Adherence to audit procedures
- **Documentation Review**: Work paper quality and completeness

## Sampling Methodologies

### Statistical Sampling
**Application:**
- Large populations of transactions or records
- Quantitative data requiring statistical validity
- Compliance testing with measurable error rates

**Methods:**
- **Simple Random Sampling**: Equal probability selection
- **Stratified Sampling**: Population divided into subgroups
- **Systematic Sampling**: Fixed interval selection
- **Cluster Sampling**: Population divided into clusters

### Judgmental Sampling
**Application:**
- Small populations or unique items
- High-risk areas requiring focused attention
- Qualitative assessments and expert judgment

**Methods:**
- **Directed Sampling**: Focus on high-risk or problem areas
- **Haphazard Sampling**: Non-random selection based on auditor judgment
- **Block Sampling**: Sequential selection of items
- **Discovery Sampling**: Focus on finding exceptions

### Sample Size Determination
**Factors Considered:**
- Population size and homogeneity
- Acceptable risk levels (confidence intervals)
- Expected error rates and materiality thresholds
- Resource constraints and time limitations

## Evidence Evaluation and Analysis

### Sufficiency Assessment
**Criteria:**
- **Quantity**: Adequate volume to support conclusions
- **Quality**: Reliable and relevant to audit objectives
- **Independence**: Not biased by management influence
- **Timeliness**: Current and representative of audit period

### Corroboration Requirements
- **Multiple Sources**: Corroborate findings with different evidence types
- **Independent Verification**: Verify through alternative methods
- **Consistency Checks**: Ensure evidence consistency across sources
- **Negative Confirmation**: Test for absence of expected conditions

### Exception Documentation
**Exception Handling:**
- Document all exceptions and anomalies found
- Assess impact and significance of exceptions
- Determine if exceptions represent systemic issues
- Recommend additional testing if needed
